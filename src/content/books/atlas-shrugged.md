My first real job was working in Product Innovation at ZF in Lafayette, Indiana. My boss at the time was Dan Williams. I have so much respect for Dan especially because of his love of learning and in particular reading. Since Dan is such a big reader, naturally I asked him what books he recommends. He smiled and told me to read Atlas Shrugged and to just start with that. I was a little thrown off that Dan would only give me one book to read, but when I found Atlas Shrugged at the library, I quickly understood why. This book is massive!

I do not read much fiction (and admittedly I still do not). And before reading Atlas Shrugged, I saw little value in fictional works because... well they did not actually happen. But interestingly enough as I read the book, I started seeing a lot of parallels to what Rand describes unfolding in real life. And I started understanding a lot more why fictional works are so important and influential.

At the end of the day, Rand helped me discover the importance of taking responsibility for actions and not blaming other people or society as a whole for life's shortcomings. She gives a strong and often controversial take that it is better to be loved for the value that one adds to a person's life than to be loved because it is a moral obligation. 

I have heard complaints of flat, 2D characters and that there is a stark dichotomy displayed in the characters who are presented. And while character attributes may be overstated, plots mildly unrealistic, or the author ranting on for a few pages longer than she should have, Rand really drives the point home that she wants to make. 

Would highly recommend giving it a read. It seriously changed me. And I mean, who is John Galt?
